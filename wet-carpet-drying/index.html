Wet Carpet Drying in Glenview

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. For Glenview homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Warning Signs

  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Swelling in the baseboards, or bubbled paint just above them
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
Our Process

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Mold has often reached the subfloor and the bottom of the drywall, so the job grows.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Soaked Pad Underneath

A carpet pad absorbs many times what it weighs in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. Such spots are frequently there for good, while the legs soak up water and swell.

Mold on the Backing

Growth usually starts on the underside of the carpet, where it is dark and damp. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.
